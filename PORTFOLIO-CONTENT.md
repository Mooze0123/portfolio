## Project 02 — Rubber Band Toy

### Metadata

- Page title: `Rubber Band Toy — Oskar Junebrink`
- Description: `An individual university project designing and fabricating a wooden tabletop toy using only wood and rubber bands, without glue, nails or other fasteners.`
- Open Graph title: `Rubber Band Toy — Oskar Junebrink`
- Open Graph description: `A wooden tabletop game that turns a strict material and assembly brief into its central play mechanic.`

### Hero

- Eyebrow: `Individual university course project · 8 weeks`
- Title: `Rubber Band Toy`
- Supporting line 1: `A tabletop ball game developed around a strict material and assembly brief.`
- Supporting line 2: `Wood provides the structure; rubber bands become the toy’s springs, pivots and visible points of interaction.`
- Hero image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/cover.jpg`
- Hero image alt: `Finished wooden Rubber Band Toy with launchers, targets and wooden balls`

### Project overview

- Heading: `Turning a restrictive material brief into the central play mechanic.`
- Paragraph 1: `The assignment was to design a toy for a specific person using rubber bands and only one other material. No glue, nails or other mechanical fasteners were permitted. I chose my sports-loving cousin as the user and developed a compact tabletop ball game built from wood.`
- Paragraph 2: `Rubber bands are not decorative: their spring-like behavior powers the launchers and creates the tactile action of play. The remaining parts use interlocking forms and careful fit to make a complete, functional object within the assembly constraints.`
- Facts:
  - Project type: `University course project`
  - Team: `Individual`
  - Duration: `8 weeks`
  - User: `Sports-loving cousin`
  - Materials: `Wood and rubber bands`
  - Methods & tools: `Sketching, physical prototyping, woodworking, laser cutting`

### 01 — Concept & constraints

- Heading: `A familiar competitive game built from an unfamiliar rule set.`
- Paragraph 1: `Designing for a sports-loving user suggested a game with immediate physical feedback, simple competition and enough variation to invite repeated play. The concept developed into two opposing launchers, a shared playing field and central targets.`
- Paragraph 2: `The strict assembly brief shaped the architecture from the beginning. Each component needed to contribute to play while also fitting into a structure that could be assembled without permanent fasteners.`
- Context image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/context.jpg`
- Context alt: `Rubber Band Toy arranged in a colorful play environment`
- Context caption: `Finished toy in a play setting`
- Play-context image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/play-context.jpg`
- Play-context alt: `Wooden tabletop toy surrounded by art materials and games`
- Play-context caption: `A compact setup designed for tabletop play`
- Early prototype image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/early-prototype.jpg`
- Early prototype alt: `Early white prototype testing the toy layout with rubber bands`
- Early prototype caption: `Early prototype used to test the layout and elastic mechanism`

### 02 — Mechanism & fabrication

- Heading: `Making tension, fit and joinery do the work.`
- Paragraph 1: `Prototyping focused on elastic response, launcher travel and how the wooden balls moved through the field. The rubber bands had to provide enough force to make the game lively while remaining easy to operate.`
- Paragraph 2: `The final structure was developed as interlocking wooden parts. Laser-cut joints established the main enclosure, while shaped wooden components created the launchers and targets. The result avoids adhesives and metal fasteners while keeping the mechanism visible and understandable.`
- Mechanism image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/mechanism.jpg`
- Mechanism alt: `Close view of a rubber band connecting a wooden mechanism`
- Mechanism caption: `Rubber-band connection within the mechanism`
- Laser-cut parts image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/laser-cut-parts.jpg`
- Laser-cut parts alt: `Laser-cut plywood panels and interlocking joints during fabrication`
- Laser-cut parts caption: `Interlocking parts during fabrication`
- Front view image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/front-view.jpg`
- Front view alt: `Front view of the completed toy showing opposing launchers`
- Front view caption: `Opposing launchers and the shared play field`

### 03 — Final prototype

- Heading: `A tactile two-player toy designed to be played again and again.`
- Paragraph 1: `Players use the two rubber-band launchers to send wooden balls toward the central target openings. The mirrored layout creates a direct, social interaction, while the unpredictable movement of the balls keeps each round active.`
- Paragraph 2: `Exposing the bands makes the source of movement easy to understand. The wooden base contains the game, supports every mechanism and gives all the loose parts a clear place within one compact object.`
- Action image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/action.jpg`
- Action alt: `Two people operating the toy as wooden balls move through the air`
- Action caption: `The launchers translate hand movement into elastic energy`
- User-testing image: `assets/images/rubber-band-toy/user-testing.jpg`
- User-testing alt: `Person playing with the completed wooden tabletop toy`
- User-testing caption: `Testing the finished interaction in context`

### Reflection

- Quote: `The material restrictions became a design tool: they forced every joint and interaction to earn its place, while the rubber bands developed from a constraint into the toy’s defining behavior.`

### Overview

- Heading: `One clear place for healthcare`
- Paragraph 1: `For this university project, our team was asked to create a functional app prototype. We chose to rethink the Swedish digital healthcare experience by bringing appointments, prescriptions, medical records, messages and reliable health information together in one place.`
- Paragraph 2: `DocBot acts as a guide through that system. Its conversational assistant helps users find the right information and understand their next step, while the main interface keeps essential healthcare services close at hand. The concept focused on clarity, accessibility and reducing the effort required to navigate several separate services.`
