Story of the Stone Golem

A magnetic construction toy
built for a fairytale world.

A sketch-to-screen exploration of form, material, movement and storytelling in Blender.

Story of the Stone Golem · case studyEnter the story ↓

Project framing

One toy, a whole fairytale.

The brief was to create and visualize a toy within the fairy tale category. I wanted the object to feel playful in the hand, but also strong enough to become the hero of a complete Blender world.

That tension shaped the project: magnetic stone pieces become a golem, a small house gives him a home, and a short animation turns the product render into a story. I used the project to challenge my Blender skills and develop them through one connected narrative.

01 / Inspiration & first sketches

A fairytale world starts with a feeling.

The brief was to create and visualize a toy within the fairytale category. I began with a mood board, then sketched ideas that could become playful Blender animations.

02 / Concepts

Three directions, one world.

Three concepts moved on from ideation because each offered a different way to explore Blender: a portal, a dragon and a modular stone golem.

A doorway into play.

A magical portal turned the toy into a small scene, with the child’s hands completing the story.

A creature that hatches.

The dragon explored a character-led interaction: crack open an egg and discover what grows inside.

A character in pieces.

Every limb could be assembled, moved, taken apart and rebuilt.

I moved forward with the golem because it gave me the richest Blender challenge. I had a good story in mind and wanted to create a fun world around it—one where the toy could be understood as both a product and a character.

03 / Modeling

Building the golem and its home.

I built the base from cubes and large meshes, then used the line project tool and sculpting passes to shape the rocks. To challenge my modeling skills further, I also built the cabin that would become the golem’s home.

↓ refine
↓ weather and texture

With the forms refined, I moved into materials and texturing. Rock textures and a remapped UV layout made the stones read as one tactile material, while chipped surfaces, bump detail and vegetation gave the house a worn, lived-in quality.

04 / Advanced rigging & animation

Giving twenty stones one believable body.

Once the model was working, I added bones so I could control it. Inverse-kinematics controls keep the limbs responsive while preserving the weight of the separate stone pieces.

05 / The product

Build, break and rebuild anything.

The final toy turns induced magnetism into an open-ended play system. A build plate gives the stones a stage, but imagination decides what they become.

06 / Packaging

The story continues on the box.

Packaging makes the magnetic system legible before the first piece is touched: power on, build, explore and start again.

07 / The two rooms

From playroom to everyday ritual.

Seeing the toy in different rooms helped me test its scale and personality: a creative object on a desk, an invitation to play in a child’s room and a clear proposition in a store.

08 / The poster

A process map disguised as a storybook artifact.

When the work became dense, the poster gave every decision a clear place in the story.

Open full poster PDF ↗
Reading the work

Three iterations moved the composition from a crowded collection of outputs toward a controlled reading path. The final poster keeps the parchment language while using the golem’s trail to guide the eye through concept, modeling, animation and product.

09 / Final video

A fairytale world you can enter.

The final landscape film brings the toy, the house and the movement together in one quiet story.

A product visualization with a pulse.

The moving light, trees and grass give the environment a little life around the golem. This is where the original toy brief became a world I could walk into.

“The project started with a toy brief. It grew into a fairytale world that made me better at modeling, materials and motion.”
